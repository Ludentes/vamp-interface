## Do learned face-generator latent spaces overlap with FLAME and ARKit/FACS?

The short version: there is partial, lossy, and surprisingly stubborn overlap between learned face-generator latents (StyleGAN W/W+, face-vid2vid/LivePortrait implicit keypoints, FLAME-conditioned NeRF/3DGS avatars) and the engineered vocabularies humans built for faces (FLAME's shape/expression bases, the Facial Action Coding System / ARKit's 52 blendshapes). The overlap is good enough that you can read AU intensity off a StyleGAN W code with linear probes, and you can drive a StyleGAN with a 3DMM rig — but it is not good enough that FLAME/ARKit are the natural axes of the data. They sit at an awkward angle to what the network actually learned, and that mismatch shows up as entanglement, identity drift, and (the load-bearing point for our application) collapse on stylized anchors.

### Linear AU probes and SVM hyperplanes mostly work

The cleanest evidence for partial alignment is that very simple probes succeed. InterFaceGAN [1] trains per-attribute linear SVMs over W and reports that for binary attributes (smile, pose, age, gender, eyeglasses) a single hyperplane separates >95% of validation samples — i.e., these attributes are linearly readable from W. Nitzan et al.'s LARGE [2] extends this from binary to continuous regression and shows that distances from such hyperplanes calibrate well to real-world values with only a few labels, including for AU intensities. AUEditNet [3] manipulates 12 AUs in StyleGAN's W+ with a dual-branch architecture trained on only 18 subjects and reports comprehensive AU/identity disentanglement, which would not be possible if AU axes were highly curved or globally tangled.

That is the "good news" half of the picture. The "bad news" half is in the same papers. Zindancıoğlu & Sezgin's perceptually-validated study [4] explicitly demonstrates that naive latent-direction edits couple conceptually distinct AUs: in their words, "brow lowerer and lip tightener are distinct action units, they appear correlated in the training data, and StyleGAN has difficulty in disentangling them." They have to fall back to spatial masking (compute a "region of influence" per AU and clamp the edit there) to get disentangled control. So AU axes exist in W, but they aren't the canonical basis — they are oblique to the directions the generator actually carved out, and you can't separate them without out-of-band help (masks, supervised heads, contrastive losses, or 3DMM cycles).

This pattern is consistent across the unsupervised-direction literature. GANSpace [5] and SeFa [6] discover interpretable directions without supervision: many turn out to be familiar (pose, smile, age, lighting, glasses), a few correspond to individual AU-like motions (mouth open, eye close), but most discovered directions are entangled clusters that don't map to any AU at all. To my reading, no published unsupervised-discovery method has produced a basis that is one-to-one with the FACS AU set — only a handful of AU-like axes show up among many other directions the network found more salient (lighting tilt, hairline, camera azimuth). This is a *negative* result for the strong "AUs are natural axes" hypothesis, and the literature is largely silent on quantifying how many of the top-N unsupervised directions match AUs vs. don't.

### Rigging works but only by going through visual intermediates

The StyleRig / GIF / PIE / MOST-GAN line of work asked the explicit question: can we drive a StyleGAN-style generator with a 3DMM (FLAME, BFM)? StyleRig [7] succeeds by training a RigNet that maps 3DMM semantic parameters into StyleGAN's W, using a self-supervised cycle through a differentiable face renderer. The trick is that StyleRig never asks the generator to consume 3DMM parameters directly — it learns a translator. PIE [8] builds on StyleRig for real-image embedding+editing of pose/expression/illumination. MOST-GAN [9] goes further by *architecturally* baking a non-linear 3DMM (shape, albedo, pose, lighting) into the generator, achieving disentanglement-by-design at the cost of training a new model.

The most directly informative finding is GIF's [10]: when Ghosh et al. tried to condition StyleGAN2 *directly* on FLAME parameter vectors, results were "unsatisfactory"; conditioning on a *rendered* FLAME geometry buffer worked well. Direct parameter conditioning fails, intermediate-image conditioning succeeds. This is strong evidence that FLAME parameters are not naturally aligned with the generator's input expectations — the generator wants pixel-space hints, not abstract shape/expression coefficients. Disentangled3D [11] and StyleMorpheus echo this: 3D-aware generators need explicit architectural disentanglement to get clean geometry/appearance separation; it doesn't fall out for free.

For our use case, the takeaway is uncomfortable: the most reliable way to drive a learned generator with FLAME/ARKit is to rasterize FLAME first, then condition on the render. That is exactly what GAGAvatar [12], HAvatar / HiDe-NeRF [13], VOODOO 3D [14], and Portrait4D do — they take FLAME pose+expression, render some intermediate (tri-plane features, normal maps, NeRF density), and let the generator hallucinate identity and detail on top. The FLAME signal is a structural anchor, not a faithful description. The renderer fills in eyebrows, micro-expression, eye-corner crinkles, sclera shading, hair motion — none of which FLAME's ~100-d expression space can specify.

### Implicit keypoints (face-vid2vid, LivePortrait): not FLAME, not AU, but *AU-ish*

LivePortrait [15] inherits the implicit-keypoint design from face-vid2vid [16]. Both papers are explicit that their keypoints are unsupervised: face-vid2vid notes that "the estimated keypoints do not embody explicit semantics" and that the framework decomposes identity and motion without any 3DMM, AU, or landmark supervision. LivePortrait adds that "compact implicit keypoints can effectively represent a kind of blendshapes" and, separately, uses 2D landmarks during training as auxiliary guidance to encourage the implicit keypoints to capture micro-expressions. This is the most candid statement in this corner of the literature: implicit keypoints behave *like* blendshapes operationally (you can linearly recombine them to drive expression) but they are not aligned with any specific blendshape standard. To my knowledge, no published work systematically regresses FLAME or ARKit b_61 parameters from LivePortrait's implicit-keypoint vector and reports per-channel R²; this is a gap.

Where LivePortrait's keypoints *do* obviously fail to be FLAME-aligned is geometry: they don't separate identity-shape from expression cleanly the way FLAME does by construction. This shows up downstream as the "head pumping" and scale-drift artifacts we've documented in our own bridge work (MEMORY: project_lp_streaming_recipe.md), which are essentially the implicit-keypoint motion code leaking shape information that FLAME would have factored out. Our `--scale_clamp 0.0 --smooth_motion` workaround is, viewed this way, manually re-imposing a piece of FLAME-style factorization that the implicit-keypoint basis doesn't enforce.

### What this means for our renderer choice

For the four conditioning options the brief raised — (a) FLAME, (b) ARKit b_61, (c) LP-style implicit keypoints, (d) combinations — the literature suggests:

- **FLAME alone** is high-fidelity for shape/pose but low-fidelity for expression (its expression PCA has ~100 dims trained on ~hundreds of subjects [11]). It works only if you also condition on rendered intermediates [10, 12].
- **ARKit b_61** has the inverse profile: well-engineered for expression (designed to drive blendshape rigs in real time) but with no shape model. Direct conditioning on ARKit coefficients has the same failure mode as direct FLAME-parameter conditioning [10]: the generator doesn't speak that language.
- **Implicit keypoints (LP/face-vid2vid)** are what generators *actually* prefer to be driven by — they are in-distribution. The cost is no interpretability and no clean shape/expression factorization, which is exactly what bites on stylized anchors where you want to preserve the painter's identity geometry while transferring only expression.
- **Combinations** — FLAME shape as anchor, ARKit/AU as expression delta, implicit-keypoint residual for everything FLAME+ARKit can't say — is where current SOTA avatar work (GAGAvatar, VOODOO XP, Portrait4D) is converging. Quality/editability tradeoff: the more interpretable the basis, the more identity drift and the worse the stylized-anchor behavior; the more implicit, the better the photorealism and the worse the controllability.

The stylized-anchor question deserves a flag: the only paper I found that directly addresses 3DMM fitting on cartoon/caricature faces [17] reports that standard FLAME fits give correct overall shape but lose fine structure (especially eyes), and required a second Laplacian-deformation stage to handle exaggerated features. By extension, *any* FLAME- or AU-conditioned renderer is going to fight stylized inputs, because the conditioning basis was never built to represent them. This is consistent with our own falsified results on PersonaLive collapsing on Pushkin-painting anchors that LivePortrait handles cleanly (MEMORY: feedback_bridge_is_artifact_source.md): the more your conditioning leans on FLAME/AU vocabulary, the less robust it is to non-photoreal anchors.

### The vocabulary question

So: do FLAME and FACS correspond to natural axes in the data, or do they organize the world for human convenience? The evidence above points firmly to the second answer with a partial-credit asterisk. AUs are *partially* recoverable as linear directions in W — about as well as "smile" or "age" — but they are not the basis the network would have chosen, and adjacent AUs alias on each other in ways that track training-set correlation rather than facial anatomy. FLAME is an excellent compression of human-face geometry by human standards (interpretable, low-dimensional, animator-friendly) but it is a *projection* of the data manifold, not its tangent. The fact that no purely-learned generator independently reinvents FLAME's basis under unsupervised analysis (only fragments of it [5, 6]) is the cleanest evidence I have that these vocabularies are human conveniences first and natural axes second.

For our renderer, this argues for treating FLAME/ARKit as *measurement* and *anchoring* layers (where they are strong) and leaving the actual generation in the implicit-keypoint regime (where the generator is comfortable). The bridge between them — distilling AU/FLAME readouts from implicit motion codes, or injecting AU residuals on top of implicit-keypoint drive — is exactly the contested gap in current work, and where our application has room to contribute.
